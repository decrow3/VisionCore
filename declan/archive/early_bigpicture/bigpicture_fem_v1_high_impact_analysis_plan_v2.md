# High-impact FEM/V1 analysis plan, v2

**Purpose:**  
This document defines the next analysis phase for the FEM-driven V1 geometry project. The goal is to prioritize analyses whose outcomes could change the interpretation of V1, active sensing, and cortical variability, rather than accumulating merely interesting observations.

This version incorporates the updated strategy: keep the high-impact synthesis alive, avoid overreacting to single null results, and predefine what counts as headline-worthy, supporting, or null for each Phase 1 analysis.

---

## 0. Operating principle

The project should not narrow itself every time one operationalization fails. Individual analyses can be underpowered, parameter-sensitive, or testing the wrong object. The finite-difference-step reversal in the Jacobian smoothness work is the cautionary example.

At the same time, the project should not treat the ambitious synthesis as proven. The plan below distinguishes between:

1. **Already-supported cautious framing**
2. **High-impact candidate claims**
3. **Adjudicating tests**
4. **Decision criteria for moving forward or falling back**

---

## 1. What is already supported

The cautious version of the project is already viable:

> Fixational eye movements generate a measurable, image-conditioned component of foveal V1 population covariance. This component is partially predicted by retinal-reafference geometry in a digital twin. In the model, retinal translation induces structured finite-displacement geometry, and at fine E-optotype scales drift-sampled phases contain more identity signal than central fixation under energy-like pooling.

This is enough for a focused paper, but the project may contain a broader result.

---

## 2. The highest-impact synthesis we are testing

The strongest version now worth aiming at is:

> A substantial component of V1 shared variability during fixation is not intrinsic noise but image-conditioned reafferent covariance generated by self-motion. This covariance reflects the population derivative of the viewed image under retinal translation, and what remains after accounting for it may reveal the intrinsic decorrelating structure of the cortical circuit. Thus, shared variability in foveal V1 is partly a structured active-sampling signal rather than nuisance variability around a static code.

This is the version that would matter beyond the project. It connects:

- V1 population coding,
- active sensing,
- noise correlations,
- efficient coding/decorrelation,
- equivariant representation,
- and the interpretation of trial-to-trial variability.

# Branch A: FEM correction reduces positive shared variability

## Candidate claim

Conventional positive V1 noise correlations during fixation are partly caused by unmodeled self-motion. After accounting for FEM-induced reafferent covariance, corrected residual correlations are reduced but remain positive under matched preprocessing.

## Why it matters

This still changes how the field interprets noise correlations in awake V1 by separating reafferent shared variability from intrinsic variability. The current supported statement is:

> FEM correction reduces positive shared variability, but does not yet support a robust intrinsic-decorrelation sign-flip claim.

## Manuscript role

Likely main figure if it replicates. This is the broadest-impact route.

## Required analyses

1. Replicate raw → FEM-corrected noise-correlation change across sessions.
2. Compare raw, PSTH-corrected, eye-conditioned, model-predicted, and residual correlations.
3. Run synthetic controls to test whether correction procedures induce negative residuals.
4. Relate residual correlations to tuning similarity, receptive-field overlap, cortical distance, firing rate, and reliability.
5. Compare the corrected residual covariance to efficient-coding/decorrelation predictions.

## Predefined outcomes

### Headline-worthy

- Deprecated for current branch state unless future evidence changes sign under matched preprocessing.
- Keep this as a possible supplement path only if robust sign-flip evidence is found later.

### Supporting

- Robust reduction in positive noise correlations with corrected medians still positive.
- Synthetic controls (including estimator-specific controls) rule out major artifacts.
- Result supports reafferent covariance reduction framing, not intrinsic decorrelation.

### Null

- Reduction is small, inconsistent, or explained by estimator/correction artifacts.
- Do not claim decorrelation or sign flip. Retain only conservative shared-variability correction framing.

## Branch freeze note

- Reconciliation branch is closed for now.
- Keep all reconciliation artifacts and corrected labels.
- Do not spend additional time searching for the historical +0.0492 -> -0.0179 source unless explicitly needed for a supplement.

## Pros

- Directly engages major literature.
- Uses existing data.
- Potentially strong single-number result.

## Cons

- Methodologically delicate.
- Requires strong artifact controls.
- May become a separate paper if it expands too much.

# Branch B: low-dimensional image-conditioned derivative geometry

## Candidate claim

FEM-induced covariance is not a global eye-position signal. It is an image-conditioned derivative code: a low-dimensional population representation of how the currently viewed image changes under retinal translation.

## Why it matters

This is the core geometry claim. If true, it reframes FEM covariance as the population derivative of the image, not nuisance variability.

## Manuscript role

Likely main figure. Provides mechanism for Branch A.

## Required analyses

1. Estimate FEM covariance dimensionality in model and empirical data.
2. Compare:
   - global eye-position basis,
   - shared translation basis,
   - image-specific translation basis,
   - shared-plus-image basis,
   - model `B_model`,
   - model `FEM_PCs`,
   - local/finite-difference derivative basis.
3. Test image-shuffled and eye-shuffled controls.
4. Quantify content/transformation separability:
   - identity/content subspace,
   - translation subspace,
   - overlap and mimicry.
5. Relate covariance magnitude/orientation to image statistics:
   - local gradients,
   - contrast,
   - spatial frequency,
   - edge orientation,
   - phase.

## Predefined outcomes

### Headline-worthy

- FEM covariance is low-dimensional, close to 2D or strongly dominated by 2 translation-related dimensions.
- Image-specific basis explains substantially more covariance than global/shared eye-position basis.
- Model derivative or finite-displacement bases align with empirical covariance above image/eye shuffles.
- Alignment survives split-half reliability normalization.

### Supporting

- FEM covariance is low-dimensional and model-aligned, but mainly shared with image-dependent gain modulation.
- Supports equivariant geometry, but not strong image-specific derivative-code claim.

### Null

- FEM covariance is low-dimensional but not model/derivative aligned.
- Or image-specific basis does not outperform global/shared basis.
- Retain empirical bridge but weaken derivative-code framing.

## Pros

- Tests the central synthesis directly.
- Links model and neural data.
- Could unify B_emp, B_model, FEM_PCs, and covariance results.

## Cons

- Needs careful control for image identity leakage.
- Empirical image-specific estimates may be reliability-limited.
- Interpretation depends on model fidelity.

# Branch C: joint causal state and trial-average reinterpretation

## Candidate claim

The response of V1 during fixation is not a stationary response to image identity plus noise. It is a structured response to the joint causal state: image content, retinal pose, and recent eye trajectory. Standard trial averaging across nominally identical stimuli averages over different retinal states.

## Why it matters

This extends the project from a specific FEM covariance result to a broader methodological claim about V1 physiology.

## Manuscript role

Potential supporting figure or conceptual section, depending on effect size.

## Required analyses

1. Compare response models:
   - image/time only,
   - eye state only,
   - additive image + eye,
   - image × eye interaction,
   - model-predicted retinal reafference.
2. Compare marginal tuning measures versus eye-conditioned tuning measures.
3. Test response reliability under stimulus-time grouping versus eye-trajectory-similarity grouping.
4. Quantify how much "unexplained variability" disappears when the state variable is retinal pose rather than nominal stimulus time.

## Predefined outcomes

### Headline-worthy

- Image × eye interaction explains substantial variance beyond additive image and eye terms.
- Eye-conditioned grouping produces much higher reliability or qualitatively different tuning.
- Standard trial-average metrics change meaningfully under eye conditioning.

### Supporting

- Eye conditioning improves response prediction/reliability modestly.
- Useful as a methodological implication, not main claim.

### Null

- Additive eye correction captures most effects, or improvements are small.
- Do not claim trial averages are fundamentally misleading.

## Pros

- Broad implication for standard V1 analysis.
- Directly connected to active-sampling framing.

## Cons

- Could be dismissed as "control eye position."
- Needs large effect to matter.
- May overlap with earlier McFarland-style claims.

# Branch D: active-sensing mechanism at acuity limit

## Candidate claim

At acuity-limit scales, stabilized center responses in the model are identity-poor; fixational drift samples phases with larger identity signals; nonlinear/energy-like pooling recovers the signal. This makes FEMs computationally important, and potentially necessary, at the acuity limit.

## Why it matters

This connects geometry to function. It explains why drift could matter behaviorally, not just statistically.

## Manuscript role

Model-side mechanism figure. Could be main if the paper emphasizes active-sensing function.

## Required analyses

1. LogMAR sweep for center-vs-FEM identity magnitude.
2. Estimate crossover scale where FEM sampling begins to outperform center fixation.
3. Compare crossover scale to known behavioral acuity.
4. Orientation 90 sign-flip check.
5. Periodogram of signed identity projections versus predicted stroke frequency.
6. Off-center unit-level OSI / identity selectivity.
7. Optional: counterfactual dither traces.

## Predefined outcomes

### Headline-worthy

- Center-vs-FEM crossover occurs near behavioral acuity scale.
- Sign-flip period tracks stimulus stroke frequency across orientation/scale.
- Off-center phases recover identity signal at unit or population level.
- Energy-like pooling explains the benefit better than signed linear integration.

### Supporting

- Mechanism holds for tested E conditions but is not clearly tied to behavioral scale.
- Use as model hypothesis.

### Null

- Period/sign structure fails to generalize across orientation/scale.
- Center-vs-FEM effect is stimulus-specific.
- Do not overstate active-sensing mechanism.

## Pros

- Intuitive and functional.
- Connects to active-sensing and acuity literature.
- Existing model results already suggest it.

## Cons

- Biology-side validation absent without E-optotype recordings.
- Strongest claims may be model-only.
- Risk of overstating necessity.

## 6. Phase 1 decision criteria

After completing Tier 1, decide which manuscript route is justified.

### Scenario 1: all three Tier 1 analyses land

- 2D covariance is image-conditioned and derivative-like.
- Noise-correlation correction shows large controlled reduction with corrected medians remaining positive.
- Aggregation scaling explains per-instant nulls.

**Decision:** pursue high-impact covariance/noise-correlation framing. Begin manuscript around Branch A+B.

### Scenario 2: two of three land

**Decision:** continue to Tier 2 to fill the missing piece. Manuscript may still carry ambitious framing, but with narrower claims.

Examples:
- 2D covariance + aggregation land, noise reduction remains positive: emphasize reafferent covariance reduction framing.
- Aggregation + reduction land, derivative geometry weak: emphasize correction of shared variability, avoid derivative-code claim.
- 2D covariance + reduction land, aggregation weak: retain covariance result, avoid "weak per instant, aggregates well" explanation.

### Scenario 3: one of three lands

**Decision:** likely retreat to cautious framing. Tier 2 becomes optional or targeted to rescue a specific branch.

### Scenario 4: none land

**Decision:** manuscript becomes narrower:
- empirical bridge,
- model finite-geometry,
- E-optotype mechanism,
- no broad noise-correlation reframing.

## 7. Sensitivity audit

Before locking manuscript claims, run or document a sensitivity audit.

### Items to check

1. Did any Jacobian-based analyses use the finite-difference step that caused the smoothness artifact?
2. Does step-prediction regime matrix change with FD step?
3. Is the n=20 drift-vs-all-steps null adequately powered for smaller effects?
4. Is the rank-1 identity collapse robust off-center or in richer stimuli?
5. Does empirical bridge magnitude change under sign conventions?
6. Does the 30% bridge survive alternative reliability ceilings and image/window definitions?
7. Are key results robust to unit subset, bin size, and smoothing choices?

### Purpose

This audit protects against a repeat of the FD-step reversal and prevents fragile results from becoming manuscript anchors.

## 11. Final planning statement

The next phase should answer this question:

> Is the shared variability of foveal V1 during fixation partly the low-dimensional, image-conditioned derivative of the viewed image under self-generated retinal motion?

If yes, the paper can make a strong claim about what V1 variability means. If no, the project remains meaningful but should be framed more narrowly around a partial empirical bridge and model-side active-sensing mechanism.

The goal is not to force the high-impact story. The goal is to test it directly before settling for a smaller one.
