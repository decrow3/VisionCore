# Prescription: Digital-twin analyses of reafferent-covariance *structure*

**Scope.** This document prescribes the set of digital-twin analyses that support the paper's recorded backbone — *a substantial component of foveal V1 shared variability during fixation is reafferent covariance, not internal noise* — by explaining the **structure** of that covariance. It is deliberately restricted to questions a deterministic, noiseless twin can answer well. It is explicit about how each analysis relates to things we have already tried, what it adds beyond what we already know, and where it can go wrong.

It is a companion to the recorded Results draft (*"Fixational eye movements reveal the reafferent geometry of V1 noise correlations"*). The recorded data is the paper; these analyses are the twin's contribution to it.

---

## 1. Why the twin is the right instrument here (and only here)

The recording isolates reafferent covariance empirically: conditioning the response on eye position and removing that component drops pairwise noise correlations to ~zero. So in the recorded data, reafferent ≈ total noise correlation. But the recorded reafferent component is still measured *through* spiking noise and finite trials.

The twin is deterministic. Its only source of across-trial variance is variation in eye position, so its across-trial covariance **is** the reafferent covariance, computed with no internal noise and no sampling error:

```
C_FEM(I) = E_t[ Cov_e( r(I, e, t) | t ) ]
```

mirroring the recorded `Σ_FEM = E_t[Cov_e(R(t,e)|t)]`, but noise-free. This makes the twin a clean generative model of *exactly* the component the recording isolated. The division of labour is non-overlapping: **the recording proves the reafferent covariance exists and dominates; the twin explains why it has the structure it has.** None of the analyses below requires the twin to make a performance, information, or discrimination claim.

### The organizing distinction: occupancy vs trajectory

`Σ_FEM` is an *instantaneous, across-position* covariance averaged over time. Its value depends on the distribution of eye positions sampled at each instant (the **occupancy**), not on the temporal order of any trajectory. Two eye-trace ensembles with the same per-instant position distribution but different temporal dynamics produce the *same* `Σ_FEM`.

This is the structural counterpart of the moments framing we settled on:

- **First moment** (the now-demoted discrimination benefit) is a within-trial *trajectory* average — depends on the path.
- **Second moment** (this covariance) is an across-trial *occupancy* variance — depends on the dwell distribution.

Same eye-conditioned modulation, two moments, two dependencies. This matters because it means the covariance analyses carry **no temporal-code or path-integration confound** — the failure modes that sank the first-moment work do not apply here.

---

## 2. The boundary: what we are *not* asking the twin

Stated up front because every prior cycle relapsed into it. We do **not** ask the twin:

- whether the covariance helps or hurts coding;
- whether it is information-limiting *in magnitude*;
- how much information an eye-aware decoder recovers;
- whether FEMs improve discrimination of the smallest E (established as ill-posed for a noiseless model: discrimination performance is decoder-relative with no privileged decoder absent a noise floor).

All of these are performance questions that require a noise model to be well-posed. They are deferred to the optional extension in §5, kept entirely separate from the structural results below.

## 3. Core structural analyses

Each entry: **Aim**, **What it computes**, **Relationship to prior work** (similarities / differences — explicit), **Issues & risks**, **Value beyond what we already know**, **Priority**.

Define throughout: `C_FEM(I)` = twin reafferent covariance for image `I` (above). `S` = stimulus-driven response subspace (directions along which `r` varies across images / the signal manifold). `T_I` = local retinal-translation tangent at the cloud centre, `span{∂r(I,e)/∂x, ∂r(I,e)/∂y}`.

---

### A1 — Signal-alignment is the inevitable signature of image-motion variance

**Aim.** Show that the alignment between the reafferent covariance and the stimulus-driven subspace is a *necessary consequence* of the covariance being generated by image motion, not a contingent or alarming coincidence.

**What it computes.** Subspace overlap between `C_FEM(I)` and `S`, for each image, in the twin. Independently vary "moving the image" (eye shift) and "changing the image drive" (different stimulus) — a manipulation only possible in a model — and show the directions coincide. Report the *expected* alignment for pure reafference.

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar:* the recorded analyses already measure signal-alignment (reported variously as ~0.77 earlier and 0.29 in a recent figure), and Phase 7 already argued, verbally, that FEM covariance is "not strictly information-limiting by default."
- *Different:* prior work *measured* alignment in cortex and *asserted* its benign interpretation. This analysis *derives* the alignment mechanistically by decoupling image and eye, which cannot be done in the animal. It converts an assertion into a demonstrated structural inevitability, and produces a predicted alignment value that can **adjudicate the 0.29-vs-0.77 discrepancy** (which value is consistent with pure reafference under each metric).

**Issues & risks.**
- The result hinges on how `S` is defined (PSTH/across-condition covariance vs the cross-image response manifold). Pick one definition, state it, and check robustness to the other.
- If used to bridge quantitatively to the recorded number, the eye-position / stimulus-shift coordinate-frame audit (y-sign) must be settled first or alignment will be artificially suppressed.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Reframes the single most reviewer-sensitive recorded observation. A signal-aligned noise covariance normally reads as the dangerous, information-limiting case (Moreno-Bote). The twin shows alignment is what reafference *must* look like — moving the image and changing the image drive overlapping response directions — so the recorded alignment is expected, not evidence of an information catastrophe. This is the twin's headline contribution.

**Priority: essential (lead result).**

---

### A2 — Low rank, and what sets the rank

**Aim.** Ground the recorded low dimensionality (participation ratio ≈ 2.0–2.5) in a manipulable mechanism, and explain the part of the rank that exceeds 2.

**What it computes.** Participation ratio / spectrum of `C_FEM(I)` in the twin. Then a causal manipulation: restrict the replayed eye motion to 1D vs 2D and show PR tracks the translational degrees of freedom; quantify the excess rank above 2 as a function of cloud width (a measure of response-surface curvature over the finite drift).

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar:* "low-rank because 2D translation" has been the standing verbal framing since Phase 1; twin PR values (~2.1–2.5) already exist.
- *Different:* prior work asserted the 2D explanation; this *tests* it by controlling the eye degrees of freedom, and decomposes rank into translation-DOF (≈2) plus a curvature term. It predicts how rank should change with FEM amplitude.

**Issues & risks.**
- PR is sensitive to trace count and to rate normalization; estimate with the same trace budget as the recorded comparison and report stability.
- "Curvature" needs an operational definition (e.g. rank growth of `C_FEM` as the cloud radius increases); avoid over-interpreting a continuous rate of growth as a discrete extra mode.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Turns "low-rank, as expected" into "low-rank *because* translation is 2D, with the excess quantifying surface curvature." Gives a mechanistic and predictive account (rank vs eye amplitude) the recording alone cannot provide.

**Priority: essential.**

---

### A3 — Stimulus-specificity: the covariance is not a global state mode

**Aim.** Establish that the reafferent covariance subspace rotates with image content, ruling out a behavioural-state / global-gain / arousal explanation of the recorded shared variability.

**What it computes.** Subspace overlap of `C_FEM(I₁)` vs `C_FEM(I₂)` across image pairs in the twin (low overlap ⇒ image-specific). Pair this with the existing displacement-decoding result.

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar:* this is the *same phenomenon* as the cross-image displacement-decoding analysis already run (within-image R² ≈ 0.997–0.999; cross-image R² ≈ −1.3, i.e. anti-generalizes). That analysis is essentially complete.
- *Different:* prior framing treated cross-image failure as a **null** (no universal transformation signal). Here the identical fact is repurposed as a **positive control**: image-specificity is exactly what distinguishes reafferent covariance from a stimulus-independent state mode. Same data, inverted role. The new piece is expressing it as covariance-subspace overlap across images rather than only as decoding R².

**Issues & risks.**
- Mostly repackaging an existing result; the only new computation is the covariance-subspace overlap, which is cheap.
- Don't overclaim: image-specificity in the twin supports the *interpretation* of the recorded covariance; it is not itself a recorded result.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Pre-empts the most likely reviewer alternative ("isn't this just arousal / global gain?"). A global-state mode would be image-invariant; reafferent covariance is image-specific, and we can show it. This converts a previously logged null into a load-bearing control for almost no additional work.

**Priority: essential (cheap, defensive).**

---

### A4 — The covariance subspace is the translation tangent (Jacobian, done correctly)

**Aim.** Show mechanistically that `C_FEM`'s dominant directions are the local retinal-translation directions — closing the loop from "the eye moves the image" to "this is the covariance."

**What it computes.** Alignment between the top eigenvectors of `C_FEM(I)` and `T_I` (subspace overlap). Characterize how alignment degrades as the drift cloud widens.

**Relationship to prior work.** This is the most important "explicit difference" in the document, because the Jacobian arm failed before and must not be revived in its failed form.
- *What failed (do not repeat):* the **magnitude identity** `C_FEM ≈ JΣ_eyeJᵀ`, which was off by ~90,000× because a local tangent was extrapolated over a ~10-arcmin cloud; and the "rough, rapidly rotating Jacobian field" idea, which a smoothness diagnostic **falsified** (the dominant direction is smooth across the cloud).
- *What survives and is prescribed here:* using `J` only to *define directions*, and testing **subspace alignment** (first-order, well-conditioned) — not magnitude. The falsified-roughness result is now an asset: because the dominant direction is stable across the cloud, subspace alignment is expected to hold even though the magnitude identity does not (the cloud-scale failure was magnitude/phase nonlinearity along a *stable* direction, not direction rotation).

**Issues & risks.**
- Hard guardrail: report **alignment only**. Any reappearance of a `JΣJᵀ` magnitude comparison is the old error returning.
- The local-tangent vs finite-cloud scale mismatch is real; expect and report alignment decay with cloud width rather than claiming a single tangent captures the whole cloud.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Provides the mechanistic link — the recorded low-rank, signal-aligned covariance *is* the retinal-translation footprint — using the one piece of the Jacobian framework that is well-posed, while being explicit that we are not resurrecting the identity that failed.

**Priority: strong support.**

### A5 — Structure is set by occupancy, not by FEM dynamics

**Aim.** Demonstrate that the *structure* of the reafferent covariance is determined by where the eye dwells, not by the temporal statistics of fixational motion — the structural counterpart of "second moment is an occupancy object."

**What it computes.** Recompute `C_FEM` in the twin under (i) real eye traces, (ii) random dither matched to the marginal occupancy, (iii) dither matched on amplitude but not occupancy shape. Compare rank, subspace, and alignment. Prediction: (i) ≈ (ii) ≠ (iii).

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar in name only:* "random dither controls" were specified in Phase 6 (E1) — but there they served the **efficiency / bits-per-spike** question ("is real FEM special vs matched dither for *performance*?"), which needs a noise model.
- *Different:* this is the **structural** version, asking whether the *covariance geometry* depends on dynamics or occupancy. It is well-posed for a noiseless model precisely because it is about the covariance, not performance. We are explicitly **not** running the Phase-6 efficiency dither here.
- *Connects to a prior puzzle:* the old intervention (results_summary §1.2b) found that removing the FEM subspace helped *real and stabilized equally*, because stabilized traces are each held at their own mean position and so still carry a low-rank positional nuisance. That is the occupancy story showing through — even "stabilized" has occupancy spread. This analysis formalizes and explains that observation.

**Issues & risks.**
- The prediction is close to analytic (`Σ_FEM` depends on the position distribution by construction), so present this honestly as a **confirmatory demonstration with a conceptual payoff**, not a discovery.
- Care in matching dither to occupancy vs amplitude so the contrast is clean.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Cleanly severs the covariance from any temporal-dynamics interpretation and from the (closed) trajectory-code branch. It is the structural statement that the second moment is governed by dwell, complementing the (demoted) first-moment trajectory story, and it explains the old "stabilized improves too" intervention result.

**Priority: supporting / consistency.**

### A6 — Single-neuron tuning to population covariance (the McFarland bridge)

**Aim.** Position the population reafferent covariance as the principled extension of single-neuron FEM modulation (McFarland 2016, coauthor's prior work).

**What it computes.** Define each twin unit's translation gain (its eye-position sensitivity for image `I`). Show how the top eigenvectors of `C_FEM(I)` are organized by these single-unit gains — i.e. the population covariance is the shared-input-weighted combination of single-neuron translation tuning. Optionally relate to recorded single-neuron eye-position sensitivity.

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar:* McFarland 2016 established single-neuron FEM-linked correlation; the present paper's recorded result is its population-level analogue.
- *Different:* this is a **new** twin analysis that explicitly constructs the single-neuron → population mapping, which has not been done. It makes the "population extension of a known single-neuron effect" claim concrete rather than rhetorical.

**Issues & risks.**
- Defining "translation gain" per unit needs care (local derivative vs finite-cloud sensitivity); use the finite-cloud version to match the covariance object.
- The optional recorded comparison reintroduces the model-data bridge caveats (§4); keep the core claim twin-internal.

**Value beyond what we already know.** Connects the result to the lab's prior single-neuron finding and to the broader literature, and explains *how* single-neuron translation tuning aggregates into low-rank population structure — a mechanistic step neither the single-neuron work nor the recorded population covariance provides on its own.

**Priority: strong support (novelty positioning).**

---

### A7 — Mimicry as recoverability *geometry* (not magnitude)

**Aim.** Provide the structural map of where reafferent covariance would be confusable with stimulus identity vs separable from it.

**What it computes.** Translation mimicry — alignment of `T_I` with identity-difference directions `(μ_a − μ_b)` — per orientation pair and per retinal phase, plus the occupancy-weighted summary. These outputs largely **already exist** (validated mimicry tables on the mono Model A population).

**Relationship to prior work.**
- *Similar:* mimicry, the phase landscape, pair-specific anisotropy, and occupancy-weighting were all computed in Phase 3 and validated later.
- *Different:* the *framing* is constrained. Phase 3 flirted with mimicry as the **mechanism** of the discrimination benefit; that was demoted. Here mimicry is **geometry only** — a map of confusability — explicitly decoupled from any recoverability *magnitude* or information claim, which requires noise (§5). This analysis is mostly correct packaging of existing results, not new computation.

**Issues & risks.**
- The standing temptation is to read mimicry as "how much information is lost." It is not; it is where loss *could* occur. Keep the language geometric.
- Belongs in a supplement or as a single schematic-plus-inset panel, not as a headline (consistent with keeping geometry secondary).

**Value beyond what we already know.** Gives the structural precondition for recoverability — *where* in stimulus space retinal pose mimics identity — which sets up the optional noise-based recoverability test cleanly and supports the Discussion's "signal in the wrong coordinate frame" point without overclaiming.

**Priority: packaging existing results; supplement.**

---

## 4. Cross-cutting caveats (apply to all of the above)

- **The twin's role is explanatory, not a tight per-image quantitative match.** The model-data covariance bridge was repeatedly found "modest and audit-dependent." Claim "the twin shows *why* this structure arises and the recording confirms it has these properties," not "twin `C_FEM` reproduces recorded `Σ_FEM` image-by-image."
- **Coordinate-frame audit is a prerequisite for any quantitative bridge.** Eye-position (data) and stimulus-shift (model) must be in compatible frames; a y-sign mismatch suppresses alignment. Settle this before A1/A4/A6's optional recorded comparisons.
- **No magnitude identities.** A4 and any tangent work report subspace alignment only; `JΣ_eyeJᵀ` magnitude is the closed, failed branch.
- **No performance language.** None of these results should be phrased in terms of accuracy, bits, discrimination, or information loss.

## 5. Optional extension (separate, requires noise): recoverability magnitude

If a functional/information claim is wanted in the paper, the **only** one worth the effort is recoverability, and it is well-posed *only* with a noise model — which is why it is separated here.

**Aim.** Quantify how much of the reafferent covariance an eye-aware ideal observer recovers relative to an eye-blind one, and show the irrecoverable residual is predicted by mimicry (A7).

**What it computes.** Add a principled intrinsic-noise model — sample the twin's Gaussian observation model, or Poisson on expected spikes — using the **eye-conditioned residual (intrinsic) variance**, *not* the total variance (the total already contains the reafferent component under study; using it double-counts). Then compare eye-blind vs eye-aware ideal-observer information about stimulus identity, and regress the recovered fraction on mimicry.

**Why separate.** Without noise, recoverability is degenerate (eye position decodes at R² ≈ 0.999, so everything is trivially recoverable). With noise it becomes a real, quantitative claim and the natural rebuttal to "is this variability good or bad for coding?" Mimicry is first-order and well-conditioned, so this avoids the p≫n trap that made the covariance *observers* unreliable.

**Status.** Optional and defensive, not load-bearing. The structural story (§3) stands without it.

---

## 6. Priorities and sequence

1. **A1, A2, A3** — the essential core. Together they give a complete mechanistic account of the recorded reafferent covariance: low-rank because translation is 2D (A2), signal-aligned because moving the image and changing the image drive overlapping directions (A1), and stimulus-specific rather than a global state mode (A3). A3 is nearly free (repackages the displacement result).
2. **A4, A6** — strong mechanistic support and novelty positioning; run after the core if the core holds.
3. **A5** — consistency demonstration; cheap, do alongside A2.
4. **A7** — package existing mimicry outputs correctly; supplement.
5. **§5 noise extension** — only if a functional claim is desired; kept wholly separate.

**What this buys the paper.** The twin stops being a contested performance model and becomes the instrument that explains *why* the recorded noise correlations have the form they do — without ever claiming a discrimination benefit. Every result above is a property of a deterministic covariance, which is exactly what a noiseless image-computable model can deliver reliably.
